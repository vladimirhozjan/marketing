**Kader 01, Engage dashboard (za 19–23 s videa), ~10 s**
1. Odpri aplikacijo, prva stran je Engage.
2. Miruj 2 s, počasi podrsaj navzdol, da se vidijo Today, Next, Waiting For, nazaj gor.
3. Konec. (Inbox števec mora biti 0.)

**Kader 02, Inbox in Quick Add (za 8–14 s), ~25 s**
1. Odpri Inbox (prazen). Miruj 1 s.
2. Tapni plavajoči gumb Quick Add.
3. Tipkaj vnose enega za drugim, po vsakem Enter, brez popravljanja:
   - Call the dentist
   - Tax report
   - Birthday gift for Ana
   - Fix the bike
   - Ask Marko about the offer
4. Zapri Quick Add, pokaži seznam petih vnosov in števec v predalu/stranski vrstici (odpri predal, če je na mobilnem skrit). Miruj 2 s.
Tipkanje naj bo naravno hitro, ne popolno; en tipkarski popravek je v redu.

**Kader 03, Clarify "Call the dentist" (za 14–19 s), ~20 s**
1. V Inboxu tapni "Call the dentist" → Clarify.
2. "Is it actionable?" → **Yes**. Počakaj 1 s.
3. "Single action or project?" → **Single action** (če korak obstaja).
4. "Can you do it in under 2 minutes?" → **No**. Počakaj 1 s.
5. Create Action: naslov je že "Call the dentist"; dodaj oznako **@phone** (naj se vidi avtodopolnjevanje); datumov ne odpiraj.
6. Shrani. Pokaži, da je vnos zdaj v Next Actions (ali da je Clarify skočil na naslednji vnos "Tax report"). Miruj 2 s.

**Kader 04, Inbox Zero (za 23–26 s), ~15 s**
1. Preostale štiri vnose preden posnameš razčisti do zadnjega ("Ask Marko about the offer" naj ostane).
2. Snemaj: odpri Inbox z enim vnosom → Clarify → Yes → No → shrani (hitro, to je le uvod).
3. Ostani na zaslonu, dokler se ne odvrti animacija "Inbox Zero. You're in control." in še 2 s po njej.

**Rezerva (po želji, ~10 s):** Next Actions z aktivnim filtrom @errands; Weekly Review "0 of 6" → prvi korak. Uporabim v petkovih videih.

I have spent most of my working life building systems that other people rely on. Telecom equipment, industrial software, and later a platform that moved a lot of money every single day. In all of that time, the hardest problem was never the code. It was keeping track of what needed to happen next.

Most of us carry that list in our heads. A call we have to make. A report that is due on Thursday. A birthday gift we still have to buy. Something a colleague promised us three weeks ago, and we are starting to wonder about it. None of these things are hard on their own. The trouble is that they all live in the same place, and that place has no search function.

So we write them down. On sticky notes, in a notes app, in the margins of a notebook, as stars in our inbox. Then we have five lists instead of one, and the head is still full, because we do not trust any of them.

That is what I wanted to solve. Not with another list, but with a system you can trust. One place where everything goes in, without sorting, without deciding. And a simple set of questions that turns each item into a next action. Is this something I actually have to do? Can I do it in two minutes? If not, what is the very next physical step? Once you answer those questions, the thing leaves your head, because you know where it lives and when you will see it again.

WhatsNextAction is that system. It is a web app, it works on your phone and on your desk, and it is free to start. You type things in, the app walks you through the questions, and what you get at the end is a short list of what to do today, what comes next, and what you are waiting on from other people. On Friday you spend fifteen minutes going through it, and nothing falls through.

It is built around a method that has been around for more than twenty years. I did not invent it. What I built is a tool that makes it easy to follow without reading a book first, because most people never will.

I will be honest about what it is not. It is not going to do the work for you. It will not guess what matters. It asks you, one question at a time, and then it remembers the answer. That is the whole idea. Less thinking about work, more doing it.
